# 5 Tips for Your First Wholesale Palm Order

Placing your first bulk order for artificial palm trees? Follow these tips for a smooth experience. Most first-order problems are not manufacturing problems — they are specification, timing or documentation problems that were avoidable before the PO was signed.

## 1. Request Samples First

Always order 1–2 samples before committing to a large quantity. We ship samples within 7 business days.

Look at three things when the sample arrives, in this order: the colour under your own lighting (not the showroom's), the fold where the frond meets the stem, and the weight of the trunk. The fold is where cheap fronds crack first; the trunk weight tells you whether the internal core is the gauge that was quoted.

## 2. Understand MOQ

Our standard MOQ is 20 units per SKU. Custom OEM orders start at 50 units. Mixed-SKU orders may qualify for reduced MOQ — ask your sales rep.

MOQ is set by the moulding and assembly setup, not by material cost, which is why it moves with custom colours and custom heights but not with quantity alone. If your project needs 12 units of one height and 30 of another, say so at the quoting stage rather than after — the mix is usually workable.

## 3. Plan for Shipping Lead Time

- Sea freight: 25–35 days
- Air freight: 7–10 days
- Express sample: 3–5 days

Add production time to these figures, not the other way round. A 500-unit order typically needs 20–30 production days before it is ready to load, and the sea transit clock starts after that.

For a fixed opening date, work backwards: installation date minus freight minus production minus a two-week buffer. If that lands in the past, air freight is the only lever left, and it is expensive per unit on bulky items.

## 4. Prepare for Customs

We provide commercial invoices, packing lists, and HS codes. Check your country's import duties for artificial plants (typically HS Code 6702).

Two things trip up first importers. First, the declared value must match the invoice — undervaluing to reduce duty is the fastest route to a customs hold and a re-assessment. Second, artificial plants are sometimes classified as "plastic articles" rather than "artificial flowers and foliage" depending on construction; confirm the code with your broker before the shipment sails, not after.

## 5. Communicate Your Project Timeline

Share your installation date with us early. We can prioritize production and recommend the best shipping method to meet your deadline.

Also tell us about site access. A palm that arrives intact but cannot fit through the atrium service lift is not a solved problem. Give us the maximum single-piece length and weight the site can accept, and we will pack and, if necessary, split the assembly accordingly.

## What Happens After the Order

Every shipment leaves with an inspection report covering colour match against the approved sample, frond attachment pull strength, and a count check. If anything is out of tolerance, it is caught before loading rather than at your site.
